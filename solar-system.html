Our Solar System

The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago. It has one star, the Sun, and eight planets that orbit it. Distances below are given in AU (astronomical units): 1 AU is the distance from Earth to the Sun.

Mercury

The smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no atmosphere, so temperatures swing wildly between day and night.

  • Diameter: 4,879 km
  • Distance from Sun: 0.39 AU
  • Year: 88 days

Venus

Covered in thick clouds that trap heat. It is the hottest planet, hotter even than Mercury.

  • Diameter: 12,104 km
  • Distance from Sun: 0.72 AU
  • Year: 225 days

Earth

The only place we know of that has life. About 71 % of its surface is covered by water.

  • Diameter: 12,742 km
  • Distance from Sun: 1.00 AU
  • Year: 365 days

Mars

The red planet. Robots have been exploring its surface for decades, and it has the tallest volcano in the Solar System.

  • Diameter: 6,779 km
  • Distance from Sun: 1.52 AU
  • Year: 687 days

Jupiter

The largest planet, a gas giant. Its Great Red Spot is a storm that has lasted for centuries.

  • Diameter: 139,820 km
  • Distance from Sun: 5.20 AU
  • Year: 11.9 Earth years

Saturn

Famous for its bright rings made of ice and rock. It is so light for its size that it would float in water, if there were an ocean big enough.

  • Diameter: 116,460 km
  • Distance from Sun: 9.54 AU
  • Year: 29.5 Earth years

Uranus

An ice giant that rotates on its side. It looks pale blue-green because of methane in its atmosphere.

  • Diameter: 50,724 km
  • Distance from Sun: 19.2 AU
  • Year: 84 Earth years

Neptune

The farthest planet, dark, cold and windy. Its winds are the fastest measured in the Solar System.

  • Diameter: 49,244 km
  • Distance from Sun: 30.1 AU
  • Year: 165 Earth years
